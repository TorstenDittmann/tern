# Split view

![Split view](split.png)

- **⌥⌘→** opens a new tab in a pane on the right, and **⌥⌘↓** opens one below. When there is already a pane that way, they move to it instead, and ⌥⌘← and ⌥⌘↑ move back.
- **⇧⌘S** (or ⌘\\, or the split button in the top bar) opens a new tab next to the current one. Press it again to end the split. **⌃⌘S** opens one below instead, and in a split it adds another pane.
- A split holds up to four tabs. **Side by Side** and **Stacked** put them in a row or a column. **Grid** shows three tabs as one on the left and two stacked on the right, and four as a 2×2 grid. **⌃⇧⌘S** (Change Split Layout) cycles through the layouts that fit, and a fourth pane switches to the grid.
- **Drag a tab from the sidebar onto the page.** Over a single page, drop it on the left, right, top or bottom half to open it there. In a split, drop it on a pane to replace that pane, or near the page's outer edge to add a pane on that side.
- **Split with…** (⌥⌘S) and **Split with Previous Tab** add to the split you're in while it has room. **Add Pane to Split** opens a new tab in it, and **Remove Tab from Split** takes the current tab out and shows it alone.
- Clicking into a pane makes it the active tab: the address bar, history, find, ⌘W, and extensions all follow it. ⌥⌘ and an arrow moves focus between panes.
- Drag the gaps between panes to resize them, and double-click a gap to even them out.
- Closing a pane takes it out of the split; the split ends when one tab is left.
- **In the tab list**, a split's tabs sit together in one tinted block (brighter while it's on screen). Each row shows a small map of the panes with its own pane filled in. Click the map to take that tab out of the split, drag the tab out of the block, or right-click ▸ **Remove from Split** or **Unsplit**.
- Switching to another tab shows it on its own. Going back to any pane restores the split. The split is saved with your session.
